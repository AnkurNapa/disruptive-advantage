AI in brewing, judged by what it saves in malt, tanks and beer

AI in brewing pays when it answers a question someone already asks and the sum behind it is written down. It fails when the model comes before the batch record.

The model is the cheap part

AI in brewing means statistical models and language models working on the records a brewery already keeps, to predict, flag or explain something a person would otherwise work out by hand. In practice that is three jobs. Forecasting a number before it happens, such as when a fermentation will reach terminal gravity. Spotting a pattern nobody asked about, such as a pump drawing more current every week. Reading text at scale, such as two years of deviation reports, then answering questions about it with the source attached.

None of that is new maths. What changed is the cost. The model is now the cheap part. The expensive part is what it always was: getting the brew number in the brewhouse log to match the tank in the cellar log, the lot on the filler and the sample in the lab book.

The model sees the number. The brewer sees the three weeks behind it: the yeast that came off a stressed generation, the gravity sample pulled before the tank was roused. AI worth paying for keeps that context attached to the number. AI that strips it off presents the wrong answer with great confidence.

What each part of the brewery sees and decides differently

CellarA predicted terminal date per tank, against the last ten batches of the same beerWhat to brew and when, with fewer idle tank days

FinanceLoss and cost per hectolitre explained batch by batchWhere next year's capital goes

Two things about that table. Every row needs data from more than one system, which is why AI projects in breweries stall at the join and not at the model. The money is also uneven. A forecast that frees one fermenter a week is worth more to a brewery running at capacity than any chatbot.

Machine learning and generative AI fail in different ways, so they need different checks. Machine learning works on numbers and is only as honest as the sensor and the definition behind each column. Generative AI works on language and is dangerous when it invents what nobody wrote. A brewery needs both. It should never let the second do the job of the first.

A worked example: what one point of brewhouse yield is worth

Say a brewery runs 400 brews a year and charges 6,000 kg of malt per brew. Brewhouse yield sits at 94 percent of laboratory extract. Analysis of mill settings, mash profiles and lauter data finds the brews that lose extract and what they had in common. The brewers act on it and yield moves to 95 percent.

Malt needed for the same extractmalt at new yield = malt at old yield x (old yield / new yield) = 6,000 kg x (94 / 95) = 5,936.8 kg

Same wort, same gravity, same volume, less grain.

That is 63.2 kg saved per brew. Over 400 brews it comes to 25,263 kg, about 25 tonnes of malt a year, before the energy and spent grain handling that go with it. The model did not save the malt. The brewer who changed the mill gap did. The model told the brewer which brews to look at.

The same shape of sum works across the plant: a small change in a rate, multiplied by a volume the brewery runs every day. That is the test for any AI in brewing proposal. If nobody can write the sum, it is a demo.

Questions to ask any AI provider before you buy

  • Which batches will it be tested on? And what would your head brewer have predicted for them without it?
  • Who changes what on the floor when it is right? If nobody can say, nothing will change.
  • Are calibration records used? A model trained on a probe reading 0.8 °C high learns the error as truth.
  • Do dumped batches appear in the history? A model that never sees the failures cannot predict them.
  • How many events is it learning from? Predicting a failure that happened twice in five years is a guess with a chart.
  • Does the language model do arithmetic? Duty, ABV and blend figures belong in tested formulas.
  • What is the sum? Ask for the money in malt, tank days or hectolitres before the first line of work starts.

Questions people ask

Can AI replace a head brewer?

No. It can point a brewer at the batches worth looking at and answer questions about the records faster. It cannot taste the beer, judge a yeast crop or know that a probe was reading high last month.

Do we need a data science team?

No. Beer365 is delivered and run for you inside your own tenant. Your brewers, engineers and finance team bring the knowledge of the plant and sign off the definitions.

Is AI worth it for a mid-sized brewery?

Only where the sum works. The two-week assessment looks for a question where a better answer is worth real malt, tank days or hectolitres. If there is none, it says so.

What data do we need first?

A brew or batch number that can be linked across the brewhouse, cellar, packaging and lab, plus the records you already keep. Most breweries hold this across several systems. Joining it is our job.
