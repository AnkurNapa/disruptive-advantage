What a brewery digital twin is

A brewery digital twin is a model of the brewery that stays in step with the real plant because it is fed by the plant's own records. At any moment it can say what is in each vessel, where it came from, how it is behaving against its plan and what is likely to happen next. The state comes from data: historian readings, brew sheets, lab results, packaging runs, the ERP. The behaviour comes from rules a brewer would recognise. Fermentation slows as gravity falls. A bright tank cannot be filled while it is being cleaned. A filler cannot package beer that has not been released.

The test is whether you can ask it a question and get an answer tied to a real batch. What is in FV7? When will it be free? A twin answers: brew 2417, pale lager, 380 hL, 6.2 °P this morning, free on Friday once the diacetyl rest and transfer are done. If it cannot answer that, it is not a twin of the brewery. It is a picture of one.

Staying in step is the hard part. Historian readings arrive every second. Lab results arrive when the lab enters them, which might be hours later. Paper brew sheets arrive when someone types them up. A good twin shows the age of each fact next to it, so the cellar manager knows the gravity is from 06:00 and the VDK result is from yesterday afternoon. Hiding that difference is how a twin loses the brewers in its first month.

What a brewery digital twin is not

  • Not a 3D render. A rotating model of the brewhouse is a nice front end. With no live data behind it, it is a screensaver. The 3D is optional. The data is not.
  • Not a simulation on its own. Textbook kinetics with no plant data show how a brewery like yours might behave. A twin shows how yours does.
  • Not a replacement for the brewer. The twin knows a tank is running two degrees warm. It does not know the glycol valve has been sticking since Tuesday, unless someone wrote it down.
  • Not a controller. A twin that reads the plant should not write to it. Valves and setpoints stay with SCADA and the operators.

We have built a twin with a 3D cellar view and we like it. The first thing visitors look at is the rotating fermenter. The first thing a cellar manager looks at is the column that says which tank is late. That tells you where the value is.

A worked example: when will FV7 be free?

Say FV7 holds 380 hL of lager at 6.2 °P this morning. The expected terminal gravity for this beer is 2.4 °P. Over the last 24 hours gravity fell 1.1 °P.

Straight-line days to terminal gravitydays = (current gravity - terminal gravity) / daily drop = (6.2 - 2.4) / 1.1 = 3.8 / 1.1 = 3.5 days

Useful as a floor. Wrong as a forecast.

Any brewer will tell you the straight line is wrong. Fermentation slows as sugar runs out and yeast settles. The last degree Plato can take as long as the three before it. A useful twin compares FV7 with the last ten batches of the same beer on the same yeast generation. If those took 5.5 days to get from 6.2 °P to terminal, the twin says 5.5 days and lists the batches it compared against.

Then it applies the rules. After terminal gravity this beer gets a diacetyl rest and a VDK check before cooling. The tank is free when the beer is transferred, not when gravity stops. A twin that forgets the rest frees the tank two days early on paper. The next brew then has nowhere to go.

Most breweries get most of the value from the first two levels. Prediction and what-if planning earn trust only after the descriptive layer has been right every morning for a while.

Questions to ask any provider before you buy a twin

  • Is every vessel bound to live data and a batch? Ask to see one tank traced to its brew.
  • Does it show how old each fact is? Gravity from this morning and a lab result from yesterday are not the same thing.
  • Who changes a rule when your process changes? If the diacetyl rest drops from 48 to 36 hours, the twin should change the same week.
  • Was it built from the P&ID or from how the plant runs today? A tank re-piped in 2019 should not show the old route.
  • Does it write to the plant? It should not.
  • What is in it on the first day? Separate what is delivered from what is on the roadmap.

Questions people ask

Is a 3D model of a brewery a digital twin?

Not on its own. A 3D model is one way to display a twin. Without live data bound to each vessel and batch, it is a render.

Does the twin control the plant?

No. Beer365 reads from SCADA, the historian and your other systems. It never writes back to control.

Where should a brewery start with a digital twin?

Usually in the cellar, where tank time is scarce: every tank linked to its batch, gravity against profile and a predicted free date. Packaging and planning come once that is trusted.

Can we change the twin's rules ourselves?

The rules sit in your tenant and belong to you. During the proof of concept we agree who changes them, so a shorter diacetyl rest reaches the twin the week it reaches the cellar.
