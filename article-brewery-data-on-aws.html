The cloud is not why the numbers disagree

A brewery that runs its ERP, historian exports or e-commerce on Amazon Web Services often hears that it has to change clouds before it can do anything useful with its data. That is rarely true. The numbers disagree because the brew number in the brewhouse log, the tank in the cellar and the lot code on the filler were never mapped to each other. That problem travels with you to any cloud.

Migration also costs a year nobody gets back. Your AWS team has built things that work. A proposal that starts by tearing them up is a proposal about the provider's stack, not about your beer. The question worth asking is narrower: what does the brewery need to see that it cannot see today and where does that data already live?

There is a people question too. The team that runs AWS should not have to learn brewing. The brewers should not have to learn AWS. Someone has to sit between the two who knows what a fermentation number is, why a blend from two tanks breaks a simple join and why the night shift's gravity readings look different from the day shift's. That is the part we bring.

How Beer365 works with a brewery on AWS

Beer365 runs on Microsoft Azure and Microsoft Fabric inside your own Microsoft tenant. For data in Amazon S3 it uses a OneLake shortcut, which is a read-only pointer to your bucket. The files stay in S3. Nothing is copied by default and nothing is written back.

  • Your stack stays put. The ERP, the historian exports and anything your team has built on AWS carry on as they are.
  • Equipment history counts too. If AWS IoT SiteWise already keeps older equipment data in your own S3 bucket, that history can be read the same way.
  • No AWS keys to hand out. Microsoft supports Microsoft Entra service principal sign-in for S3 shortcuts, so access runs through an identity your IT team controls.
  • Locked-down buckets still work. Buckets behind a firewall or VPC can be reached through an on-premises data gateway.
  • Cross-cloud reads are kept in check. Fabric can cache files read from S3 for between 1 and 28 days, which cuts repeated egress. Files over 1 GB are not cached.

The batch record, the Power BI pages, the digital twin and the copilot live in your Microsoft tenant. If you already use Microsoft 365, you already have one.

What crosses between clouds matters more than how. A day of raw historian readings can run to gigabytes. The summary of the same day, with gravity stages, peak temperatures and minutes out of profile per tank, is a small fraction of that. Beer365 reads the shaped data where it can and leaves the raw stream on AWS for the engineers who need it. That keeps the egress line on your AWS bill short.

What the brewery sees and decides differently

Once the S3 data meets the rest of the brewery in one batch record, the conversation moves from where the number is to what to do about it.

  • The head brewer sees extract per brew next to the malt lot, so a weak lot shows up after two brews rather than ten.
  • The cellar manager sees tank days per beer and the date each fermenter will be free.
  • Packaging sees OEE and dissolved oxygen by line and SKU, tied to the tank that fed the filler.
  • Quality sees results drifting against specification before they fail, with the batch history one click away.
  • Finance sees beer loss by stage in hectolitres and money, with the excise register reconciled each month.

What good looks like is easy to describe. Next quarter your AWS architecture looks the same and your cloud bill barely moves. Your morning meeting does not look the same. It runs from one set of numbers. Nobody brings a spreadsheet to argue with it.

Questions to ask any provider when you run on AWS

  • Does the proposal need a migration? If it does, ask what the brewery gains that reading in place would not give.
  • What crosses between clouds? Shaped summaries cost far less to read than raw historian streams.
  • Who pays egress and roughly how much? AWS charges for data leaving it. Get an estimate before you sign.
  • Is access read-only? Nothing should write to your buckets or your control systems.
  • Whose credentials does it use? Prefer an identity your IT team controls over long-lived access keys.
  • Where does the batch record live when the contract ends? It should already be in your tenant.

  • Proof of concept, 20 weeks. The batch record runs on your data, read in place, against a baseline signed at the start. Your plant controller signs a statement every month.

To be straight about it: a brewery with a strong AWS team can build all of this on AWS. Beer365 is for breweries that want the brewing outcome delivered and signed for, rather than a data project to staff.

Questions people ask

Do we have to move off AWS?

No. Beer365 reads your Amazon S3 data where it sits through a read-only OneLake shortcut. Your AWS stack stays as it is.

Is our data copied out of S3?

Not by default. A shortcut points at the files. Fabric can cache files for 1 to 28 days to reduce repeated cross-cloud reads.

Who pays for cross-cloud reads?

AWS charges egress to your account. Reading shaped summaries instead of raw streams keeps it small. The assessment estimates the cost before you commit.

Can our AWS team keep building on AWS?

Yes. Beer365 only reads. Anything your team builds that lands in S3 can join the batch record later.
