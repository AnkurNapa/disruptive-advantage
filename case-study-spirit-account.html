A spirit account rebuilt by hand every period

Cut points, fills and warehouse moves lived in three systems and a spreadsheet. Making the account continuous turned the duty return from a fortnight of assembly into a report that was already correct.

SectorMalt distillery
Region[REGION]
Modules04, 08
Went live[DATE]

At a glance

  • The problemThe statutory spirit account was assembled manually each period
  • What we readStill control tags, fill records, warehouse moves, ERP stock
  • What we builtA continuous spirit account and a live cask position
  • Who uses itDistillery manager, warehouse team, finance

The situation

Spirit is accounted for by law and by volume. This distillery knew its numbers, but it only knew them in arrears: cut points were logged on the still, fills were written into a warehouse book, movements were keyed into the ERP later, and the reconciliation between all three happened once a period in a spreadsheet that one person understood.

Nothing was wrong with the data. The problem was that it was assembled rather than kept. Any question asked between period ends had to wait, and the answer was an estimate until somebody had time to rebuild it.

We were not short of data. We were short of a single place where the data agreed with itself.

Distillery manager

What we read

Still control tags for charge volume, strength and cut timings. The fill records, including regauge readings taken at the warehouse. Warehouse movements and rack locations. Stock and cost from the ERP. All of it read only, in one direction, with nothing written back to anything that runs the still.

What we built

A continuous spirit account that updates as the plant runs, rather than being reconstructed at period end. Every litre of alcohol is followed from wash charge through the cut to the fill, and from the fill to its current position in the warehouse. Regauge readings adjust the account rather than being filed separately from it.

On top of that sits the excise and traceability module, which assembles the statutory return from the same record. The return is no longer a piece of work. It is a view of something that was already true.

What changed

  • The duty return became a report rather than a fortnight of assembly
  • Cask position is current, so warehouse questions are answered the same day
  • The account survives the person who used to maintain the spreadsheet
  • Yield per tonne of cereal is now visible per run rather than per quarter

Where it went next

The proof of concept ran on a simulated feed and cut over to live plant data without rebuilding the front end. The same foundation now carries yield analytics, and the plan is to bring warehouse energy onto it so maturation conditions sit alongside the account rather than in a separate system.
